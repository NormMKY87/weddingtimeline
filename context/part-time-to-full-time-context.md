## 2. Target buyer

The part-time wedding photographer with a day job who wants to go full time.

- Already shooting 5 to 15 weddings a year and earning from it.
- Has a mortgage, often kids. Risk-aware.
- Stuck on one question: can I leave my job, and when.
- Not the hobbyist looking for a creative outlet. Hobbyists are price sensitive and rarely act. They can follow the free channel and buy later.

Modelled on the Part-Time YouTuber Academy buyer (someone with a job who wants a side thing), translated to photography.

## 3. The promise

The numbers-based plan to go from side-hustle wedding photographer to full time without gambling the household income.

Differentiator: almost every photography educator teaches marketing, mindset or craft. Almost nobody teaches the actual transition decision with maths: salary replacement after tax, super and leave, cash buffer, booking count and average sale required, runway, resignation timing. Norm's paramedic to photographer arc makes him the credible teacher of that.

## 4. Frameworks borrowed

### From Ali Abdaal's Part-Time YouTuber Academy (sales page teardown)
- $995 one-time, self-paced, lifetime access, 3 month payment plan.
- Named proprietary frameworks instead of a module list (Niche Discovery Framework, HIVES, Infinite Content Engine, etc).
- Proof with numbers and timeframes (6,000 creators, case studies with subscriber counts and months).
- Bonuses that each kill a specific objection (Final Cut editing course for "I can't edit", Camera Confidence Masterclass for "I hate being on camera", community for "I'll fall off").
- 30 day money back guarantee where buyers keep the bonuses.
- The sales page has no lead magnet or webinar. All traffic comes from the free YouTube channel and the newsletter. The page only converts people who already trust him.

### From Josh McKitterick, The Program Designer (Next Level Club, 05-04 4.1)
- A course is a glorified to-do list. Pick one macro outcome achievable in 30, 60 or 90 days, list only the actions that get there.
- Each action needs one insight (the belief shift that makes the action feel safe or logical) and one tool (the worksheet).
- Six to nine actions is plenty. More than nine bits and the system is too large to hold.
- Students give two to four hours a week. A six week course is 24 hours of attention total, which must cover watching, believing, doing and troubleshooting.
- Too much content is permission to procrastinate. Extend the course or reduce the stuff.
- Slow and safe beats fast and scary. Find the lower-friction route to the same result.
- Innovate before you automate. Test live with a founding intake (not "beta"), then film once and never teach it live again.
- You need around 10 clients before you can articulate the method sharply.
- Responsible to your clients, not for them.
- Laura Higgins extended her own program from 6 to 12 weeks and cut content after applying this.

### From Laura Higgins, Course Onboarding quick start (05-04 4.6)
- Three elements after purchase: a confirmation page (excited tone, three next steps as a bullet list, a video from you), an onboarding email sequence (minimum one confirmation email with a big login button; ideally one email a week matching the action of the week), and the members platform.
- For the founding intake, one confirmation email is enough. Build the sequence from the questions students actually ask, then automate.

### From the Founders Library
- Endowed progress effect (Hooked, Nir Eyal): mark the first two actions complete the moment students join. Completion rates rise when people feel they have already started.
- Two-minute rule (Atomic Habits, James Clear): scale each first action down to something tiny.
- 12 Week Year (Brian Moran): a 90 day program with weekly scoring maps directly onto this structure.
- First 100 Days (Never Lose a Customer Again, Joey Coleman): the post-purchase period decides retention and referrals.

## 6. Flagship program: Part-Time to Full-Time

One outcome in 90 days: know your quit number and have a booking system running that is on track to hit it.

Lessons are 10 to 15 minutes, each ending with one action. Detailed finance (BAS, monthly cash flow) stays out of week one and lands in Module 5.

### Module 0: Start Here
- 0.1 Start here. My story (shifts, side weddings, the day the number said leave, the resignation) and how to use this program (one action a week, two to four hours, bonuses unlock after core actions).
- 0.2 Reply within the hour and book the call. Insight: bookings are lost to silence, not competitors.
- 0.3 Two day follow-up to-do on every lead.
- 0.4 Add one priced add-on this month (second shooter, album, travel). Average sale rises without touching base price.
- 0.5 Record your next consult and listen back. Fastest skill jump available.
- 0.6 Check the date across every calendar before confirming.
- 0.7 Outsource one cull or edit batch as a test. One wedding, one invoice.
- Worksheet: Seven Day Checklist.
- Rule: every quick win must show a measurable change inside two weeks. Nothing that needs a wedding to happen first.

### Module 1: Your Number (weeks 1 to 2)
- 1.1 Run the calculator. Set your number, gap and provisional quit date. Insight: compare net to net, never gross wedding income to a net payslip. Tool: Quit-Number Calculator. 20 minutes.
- 1.2 Build the package ladder backwards from the number, add-ons priced. Insight: 20 weddings at a higher average beats 40 at a lower one. Tool: Package Ladder sheet.

### Module 2: The Enquiry Machine (weeks 3 to 5)
- 2.1 Set up the pipeline with statuses, tags and automated follow-up to-dos. Insight: admin is a system, not a mood. Tool: pipeline setup guide.
- 2.2 Install the staged enquiry response, the date check and the competing enquiry paragraph (two couples want the same date). Insight: the first reply's only job is to book the call. Tool: enquiry sequence templates.
- 2.3 Build one lead magnet and the Instagram DM reply system. Insight: give couples a reason to message before they are ready to book. Tool: DM scripts, lead magnet brief.

### Module 3: The Consult and the Close (weeks 6 to 7)
- 3.1 Run and record a consult using the call structure. Insight: listen for budget and decision makers, do not ask for them. Tool: consult run sheet.
- 3.2 Send a proposal written off the transcript. First person singular, deliverables as dot points, exact gallery delivery dates. Insight: a proposal is a note from you, not a quote. Tool: proposal template.
- 3.3 The five objections and your replies: price pushback, budget question, still deciding, partner wants someone else, fewer hours. Norm's position on discounting.

### Module 4: The Operating System (weeks 8 to 9)
- 4.1 Install the weekly scorecard of leading and lagging indicators. Insight: you know it is working before the bank balance does. Tool: scorecard.
- 4.2 Batch the week into blocks around shifts (the 10 hour week). Tool: ideal week template.
- 4.3 Planners, venues and vendor intro emails. Send one vendor intro after every consult. Insight: planners and venues refer people who make their job easier. Tool: vendor intro email.
- This is the module no competitor can teach.

### Module 5: The Runway (weeks 10 to 11)
- 5.1 Map 12 months of cash flow with deposits landing before the work. Insight: deposits are not income yet. Tool: runway sheet.
- 5.2 Set the buffer, deposit and balance schedule, business structure and accounting basics (Pty Ltd, Xero, BAS, with disclaimer). Insight: the buffer makes the resignation safe, not the bookings. Tool: buffer and schedule calculator.

### Module 6: The Handover and the First Year (week 12)
- 6.1 Set the resignation date and the handover plan. What to tell your employer, insurance, contract check.
- 6.2 The first 90 days full time. Tool: 90 day plan.
- 6.3 When to bring on a second shooter or associate. Briefing, pay, vendor meal and travel terms, brand protection.

### Bonuses (locked until core actions are done)
- Never Lose a Wedding: working drive, archive, night-of-wedding routine. 45 minutes. Kills "I'll lose photos".
- High-End Editing and When to Outsource: editing workflow plus the outsourced retouching model with costs, turnaround and margin. Kills "my editing isn't good enough and I can't keep up".
- The Content Engine: seven beat caption framework, four or five content pieces per couple, blog system. Kills "I don't know what to post".
- The Room: community and monthly call for 12 months. Kills "I'll be alone when it gets hard".

### Craft Program (phase two, modules 7 to 11)
Framed as: shoot like a full-timer so you can charge like one.
- Module 7: The Pre-Wedding Session. Engagement session as sales tool and rehearsal.
- Module 8: The Wedding Day. Coverage model, event milestone coverage, timeline builder, light through the day, direction playbook and posing prompts, albums and print ordering. Biggest module.
- Module 9: Cultural Weddings. Preparation, who to talk to beforehand, moments that cannot be missed, family dynamics, multi-day structures, pricing and staffing. Differentiator for the sales page.
- Module 10: Shooting With a Team. Second shooters, associates, videographers. Briefing, positioning, file handoff, brand protection, choosing who to refer.
- Module 11: Client Confidence. Trust at every touchpoint from first reply to delivery. WhatsApp onboarding. Objection handling in depth.
- Needs real shoot footage. Captured across the next four weddings. Built after the business program has sold.

## 8. Decisions made

- Route two chosen over route one. Route one was a live founding intake sold with no product and no audience. Route two is the written Booking Pack first, with its buyers becoming the founding intake. Reason: it tests demand before delivery, fits the MVB rule, and gives Josh's 10 clients before filming anything.
- Flagship cut from 11 modules and 28 hours to a 90 day, nine action program. The original 28 hour design was the "teach everything" mistake Josh McKitterick names.
- Craft modules moved to a phase two program rather than diluting the business promise.
- Module 0 opens with story and quick wins, not finance. Money arrives in Module 1 as a 20 minute calculator, detailed finance in Module 5.
- Quit-Number Calculator is the lead magnet, not "25 Ways to Get More Bookings".
- Runs under Norman Yap the educator on a separate channel, list and name. NYP couple-facing brand and photographer education content never cross.
- Start with Booking Pack (written assets) rather than filming Modules 1 and 4 first. Research showed sales and consult content breaks out at nine times normal views, and written assets ship in weeks with no footage.

## 10. Rules

- Test demand before delivery. Booking Pack sells before anything is filmed.
- One outcome per program, nine actions or fewer, one insight and one tool per action.
- Two to four hours of the student's week. Extend the program or cut content, never cram.
- Quick wins first. Week zero actions must show measurable change inside two weeks.
- Bonuses stay locked until core actions are done.
- Innovate live, then automate. Teach once to the founding intake, film once.
- Separate channel, list and name from NYP.
- Pricing is Vance's call.
- Anything not on the side products list waits until the Booking Pack has sold 20 copies.

## 12. Writing and voice rules for all product copy

- No em dashes or en dashes. Use commas, full stops, parentheses.
- No semicolons. No "not just X, but also Y". No "it's not X, it's Y".
- Short sentences, active voice, specific nouns and numbers, varied sentence length.
- First person singular in anything from Norm: "I" and "my", never "we" or "our studio".
- No generic headings (Key Takeaways, Why It Matters, Final Thoughts). No rhetorical questions as transitions. No dramatic one-line fragments.
- Sound like a person with a point of view, not a summary engine.
- Client-facing: warm, human, conversational. Never templated.
