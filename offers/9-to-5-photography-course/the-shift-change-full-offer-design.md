## Part 1. The offer on one page

**Avatar.** Shift workers in emergency services and healthcare (paramedics, nurses, midwives, firefighters, police) with young kids and a mortgage, who already get asked to shoot weddings and families, and want a dated plan to step off the roster into paid photography without risking the house. Salaried parents in other fields self-select in. Hobbyists who want pocket money are told plainly this is not for them.

**The pain.** "I am trading the best years of my kids' childhood for a roster, with a skill I know could pay, and nobody who has actually done this is showing me the safe way out."

**The dream outcome, as an event.** The day they hand in their notice, with the mortgage covered and their partner relaxed.

**The promise, in three horizons.**
- In 30 days: your first paid shoot, at a price you can say out loud.
- In 12 weeks: your Exit Number, your Step Down Plan, a legitimate business and a lead system that runs around your roster.
- In 12 to 36 months: your notice, on a date you chose, with the house covered. (Range to be set from Norm's own timeline. [NORM TO CONFIRM])

**Headline.** Your first paid shoot in 30 days, and a dated plan to step off the roster without risking the house.

**The stack.** Nine named bundles worth $13,700 (Part 8): The Kitchen Table Kit, The First Booking Sprint, The Off Shift Lead System, The One Weekend Setup, The 10 Hour Week Workflow, The Exit Number Dashboard, The Step Down Plan, The First Year Playbook, The Accountability Engine.

**Vehicle.** A 12 week live cohort, capped at 15, with one to many tools (three calculators, a timeline builder, a template vault) doing most of the work. Not a self-paced course. See Part 3.6 for why.

**Price.** Founding cohort $2,497 or 3 x $897. Cohort two onward $3,497 to $3,997. Private tier $6,000 to $7,500 by application. Front end challenge $297 to $497 later. Free Exit Number Calculator as the lead magnet.

**Guarantee.** Attend the first four live calls, send the 20 outreach messages and publish your pricing. If you have not booked a paid shoot within 90 days of starting, Norm works with you one to one until you have. The exit itself is a 12 to 36 month outcome the program equips and does not guarantee, and the sales material says so.

**Name.** The Shift Change: 12 Weeks From the Roster to Paid Photographer. Front end: First Paid Shoot in 30 Days.

**Proof.** A paramedic of 15 years, father of two, who moved to full-time photography 3.5 years ago and now runs a business at roughly $300,000 a year top-line. The detailed timeline behind that sentence is the single most important asset still to be written.

## Part 2. The brief and what it implies

Facts given:

- He moved to full-time photography 3.5 years ago.
- The business now runs at roughly $300,000 top-line revenue a year.

- He has paid for many workshops, courses and business coaching programs and wants to distil what actually worked.
- The product is for someone in a 9 to 5 who is dabbling in photography and wants a clear pathway to part-time or full-time creative income.

What the brief implies (inferred):

- This is a transition and business product, not a craft product. The buyer already shoots well enough to be paid. What they lack is the map, the numbers, the permission and the proof that someone with their constraints has done it.
- The brief contains three promises in one sentence: part-time, full-time, or extra income. The method requires one.
- The brief names three possible containers: course, digital product, or tool. The method chooses the container last.

## Part 3. The book's method, run in order against this idea

This is the critical evaluation. Each step states what the book says, what the first draft of this offer did, where the gap is, and the fix.

### 3.1 Start with a starving crowd

**What the book says.** The hot dog stand story: the only advantage worth having is a starving crowd. Four indicators: massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing. Choose from the three evergreen markets (health, wealth, relationships). Do not sell resume services to the unemployed. You are not creating demand, you are channelling it.

**What the draft did.** Ran the four indicators and passed. Placed the product in wealth (career and income) with a relationship undertone (time with kids).

**The gap.** The draft passed the test at rung two of the niche ladder, where the test is easiest to pass and least useful. "Employed people who like photography" is a crowd, not a starving one. The starving crowd is narrower and hungrier.

**The fix.** Run the four indicators again at rung four:

- Massive pain: yes, and specific. Rosters, night shifts, missed bedtimes, a vocation that is eating them, a skill people keep complimenting, and a fear of being the one who talked about it and never did it.
- Purchasing power: yes. Salaried, often with penalty rates. This is the structural advantage of the avatar. Price like a career decision, not a hobby.
- Easy to target: unusually yes. Emergency services and health workers are among the most aggregated groups in the country: station culture, union groups, rostering apps, shift worker Facebook groups, graduate cohorts who stay in touch for decades, and Norm's own network from 15 years on the road.
- Growing: the pool of burnt out health and emergency workers is not shrinking (inferred, and Norm has first hand knowledge). Weddings and family photography are durable.

Verdict: a starving crowd exists at rung four. It did not clearly exist at rung two.

### 3.2 Niche down until it hurts

**What the book says.** The time management example: $19 generic, $99 for sales professionals, $499 for outbound B2B sales reps, $1,997 for outbound B2B power tools and gardening sales reps. Same content, four qualifiers, one hundred times the price. Under $10M a year, niching down almost always makes more money. Commit. Do not niche hop after one attempt.

**What the draft did.** Built a four rung ladder and recommended "build for rung four, market to rung three."

**The gap.** Marketing to rung three undoes rung four. The book's sales rep did not market to "sales professionals" and hope the power tools guy noticed. The message was for him alone. Three different buyers sit inside "9 to 5 dabbler": the hobbyist who wants pocket money, the parent who wants a safe exit, and the single 26 year old who could jump tomorrow. They have different pain, purchasing power and objections. One message cannot hold all three without going flat.

**The fix.** The ladder, with the honest trade at each rung:

| Rung | Avatar | What changes in the offer | Market size | Conviction and price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | People who want to make money from photography | Nothing. Generic course. | Enormous | Lowest. Competes with free. |
| 2 | 9 to 5 workers dabbling in photography who want to go part-time or full-time | A transition framing | Very large | Low to medium. Three avatars inside. |
| 3 | Salaried parents, roughly 30 to 45, with a mortgage, already shooting for friends, who want a staged exit into paid photography | Family and runway become central. The partner becomes a second buyer. | Large | Medium to high |
| 4 | Shift workers in emergency services and healthcare with young kids and a mortgage, already being asked to shoot weddings and families, who want a dated plan to step off the roster | Roster language, fatigue rules, secondary employment policy, the identity cost of leaving a vocation, "I am watching my kids grow up from a truck" | Small, dense, aggregated | Highest. Norm's story is the ad. Nobody can copy it. |

Decision: build, name and market for rung four. Let rung three self-select in; they will, because the story is strong enough. Rung four buyers will not buy a rung three offer with the same force. Widen only after alumni proof exists, and even then by adding a second message, not by blurring the first.

The avatar in their own words (composite, inferred, to be replaced with real sentences from Part 12):

"I am 36. I have been on the road for 11 years. I am good at my job and I am done. I shot my cousin's wedding last year and three people asked for my card. My partner is supportive but she has seen me get excited before. We have two kids under seven and a mortgage that needs my penalty rates. I know I could do this. I just do not know how to get from here to there without blowing up the one thing that keeps the house running. Everyone who teaches this either never had a real job or does not have kids."

### 3.3 Dream outcome: the vacation, not the plane flight

**What the book says.** Nobody wants a gym membership, they want to lose 20 pounds. Find the destination, state it in the customer's words, and find the status underneath it. The weight loss buyers wanted the weight off for a specific wedding on a specific date.

**What the draft did.** "I handed in my notice on my own terms. The mortgage was covered before I did it..." Good words, correct status lens.

**The gap.** It is a state, not an event. The book's buyers had a date. An undated outcome cannot be counted down to, and a program with no countdown has no urgency inside it.

**The fix.** The dream outcome becomes an event: the day they hand in their notice, with the mortgage covered and their partner relaxed. Every tool in the program counts down to that date. The Exit Number Dashboard shows how far away it is at all times.

Status lens, unchanged: responsible in their partner's eyes, the one who actually did it in their colleagues' eyes, a parent who built something in their kids' eyes, a professional in other photographers' eyes.

### 3.4 The value equation

**What the book says.** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay x Effort and Sacrifice). The buyer silently asks four questions: what will I get, how do I know it will happen for me, how long until I have it, what will it cost me beyond money. Beginners compete at the top with bigger claims. The best companies win at the bottom. People pay $25,000 for liposuction and resist $100 a month for a gym. The dotted map that shows when the train arrives beats a faster train.

**What the draft did.** Scored the alternatives (free YouTube, generic business course, business coach) and this offer, and chose to compete on likelihood and time delay.

**The gap.** The scoring was right. The draft then undercut its own likelihood score by leaving the proof as placeholders. Likelihood is a function of specificity and evidence, and the evidence is a sentence, not a timeline.

**The fix.** Keep the strategy, fill the proof. Scores, from the buyer's chair, 10 best in every row:

| Driver | Free YouTube | Generic photography business course | Business coach ($3k to $10k) | This offer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| Perceived likelihood | 3 (no proof it works for someone like me) | 4 (built by full-timers who never had a day job in the way) | 6 (generic, not photography, not family-constrained) | 9 once the timeline is written. 6 until then. |
| Time delay | 2 | 4 | 6 | 9 (paid booking inside 30 days, exit visible on a dashboard) |
| Effort and sacrifice | 2 | 4 | 5 | 9 (calculators, templates, scripts, a 10 hour week) |

The dotted map for this offer is the Exit Number Dashboard. It does not change their income. It changes how it feels to be on the way.

### 3.5 The pain is the pitch

**What the book says.** The degree of the pain is proportional to the price. Good persuasion makes the prospect feel understood, not informed. If you can articulate their pain better than they can, they assume you have the solution.

**What the draft did.** "I do not know how to get from here to there without putting my family at risk."

**The gap.** That is the customer's head voice. It informs. People pay to be relieved of a feeling, and the feeling had not been named.

**The fix.** Dig in four layers.

- Layer 1, what they say: "I do not know what to charge, how to get clients, or how to make the jump."
- Layer 2, what they mean: "I do not know if someone like me is allowed to do this."
- Layer 3, what they feel: two fears pulling against each other. The fear of jumping: "If I leave a 15 year career for a camera and it fails, I crawl back with everyone watching, and I put my family through that for a hobby." The fear of staying: "If I do not, this is my life. Another 25 years of nights, missed bedtimes, and a skill I never used. My kids will remember me as tired."
- Layer 4, the status wound: it is not about money. It is about being the person who talked about it and never did it, or the person who did it and had to come back. Both humiliations play out in front of the same audience: partner, colleagues, parents, kids.

The pain point, in one sentence: "I am trading the best years of my kids' childhood for a roster, with a skill I know could pay, and nobody who has actually done this is showing me the safe way out."

Three pitch lines built on it, to test in the conversations:

1. "You are not scared of photography failing. You are scared of being the person who left a career for a camera and had to go back."
2. "Every roster you accept is a decision. Most people never notice they are making it."
3. "You have the skill. You have the demand. What you do not have is the plan, and that is the only thing stopping you."

### 3.6 Choose the vehicle last

**What the book says.** Problems first, solutions second, delivery vehicles third. The vehicle is whatever delivers the solutions at a cost you can live with. The one to many asset built once (the spreadsheet that generated personalised meal plans in fifteen minutes and sold at premium prices for years) is where the leverage is.

**What the brief did.** Named the container first: "a course, a digital product, or a tool."

**The gap.** A self-paced course is the weakest possible container for this avatar. The two biggest problems on the list are permission and accountability, and a video cannot grant permission or notice when someone goes quiet. A course alone would sell on the story and fail on completion, and failed students are the end of the referral engine.

**The fix.** The vehicle is a live cohort with one to many tools doing the heavy lifting. The tool idea from the brief is right, in the right place: the Exit Number Calculator is the lead magnet and the spine of the program. Giving it away does not cannibalise the program, because the number is the beginning of the pain, not the end of it. A self-paced course comes later, built from the cohort recordings, as the front end challenge.

### 3.7 Where this lives

Norm works inside at least two business contexts, Norman Yap Photography and Noble & Yours. This product belongs to neither.

- Norman Yap Photography sells premium wedding photography to couples. An education product for aspiring photographers under that name muddies the message to couples and invites "he teaches now, is he still shooting" questions.
- Noble & Yours has its own positioning and this would dilute it.
- The proof in this offer is Norm's personal story. It belongs under Norm's name or a dedicated education brand.

Recommendation: a separate education brand under Norm's name, cross-promoted from the photography accounts but with its own page, list and voice. [NORM TO CONFIRM]

## Part 7. Trim and stack

**What the book says.** The sales to fulfilment continuum: the more you promise to do, the easier it sells and the harder it is to deliver. ## Part 10. Pricing and the product ladder

**What the book says.** There is no strategic benefit to being the second cheapest. Raising price raises client investment, perceived value, results, client quality and margin. Charge as much as possible while keeping the value gap enormous in the buyer's favour. Masterson's stage rule: at proof of concept, one core offer executed brilliantly.

**Core offer: The Shift Change, 12 week live cohort**

- Founding cohort: $2,497, or 3 x $897. Because: live access to someone who did exactly this, a cap of 15, and a stack worth $13,700.
- Cohort two onward: $3,497 to $3,997.
- Value to price gap stays above 4 to 1 at every step.

Why not lower: under $1,000 the avatar reads it as information, not transformation. Commitment drops, completion drops, proof never arrives, and the virtuous cycle runs backwards.

Why not higher yet: the outcome is worth a salary, so $7,500 is defensible on value. But at Stage 1 the job of cohort one is proof, and a founding price fills faster and produces the alumni results that let cohort two be priced on evidence rather than argument. Raise on proof, not on nerve.

**Backend: The Shift Change Private**

- Everything in the cohort plus four one to one calls, a personal Exit Number build, and Norm in their corner the week they resign.
- $6,000 to $7,500. Two or three people per cohort, by application. Taken straight from the 10x test.

**Front end (after cohort one has produced proof): First Paid Shoot in 30 Days**

- Self-paced version of bundle two plus the First Price Calculator and starter contract.
- $297 to $497. Its only job is to produce a paid booking and a believer who then joins the cohort.

**Lead magnet: The Exit Number Calculator**

- Free. Captures the single most emotionally loaded number in the buyer's life.

**Presentation order.** Private first, then the cohort, then the challenge. Expensive first. Anchor the $13,700 stack before any price is shown. Always give the "because".

## Part 12. Pre-sell before you build

**What the book says.** Sell it, then fulfil it. Try one hundred offers and you will succeed. Most people never try one.

1. Write the one page offer: avatar, promise, the nine bundles, price, guarantee, start date.
2. Have ten conversations with people who match rung four. Thirty minutes each, recorded. Questions below.
3. Rewrite the problem list and the objection map in their words.
4. Open a founding cohort of 12 to 15 at $2,497 with a start date eight weeks out, in Norm's quietest season.
5. Fill it from Norm's own world first: former colleagues, the photographers who already DM asking how he did it, past second shooters and assistants, workshop contacts. [NORM TO CONFIRM where these conversations already happen]
6. Deliver live, week by week, building each bundle the week before it is taught. Record everything. The recordings become the front end.
7. Track two numbers for every student: days to first paid booking, and progress toward their Exit Number. Those two numbers are cohort two's proof.
8. Collect testimonials at the first booking, not at the end.
9. Raise the price for cohort two.

**The ten conversations**

1. Tell me about the last shift that made you think about leaving.
2. When did you last get asked to shoot something for money? What did you say and what did you charge?
3. What have you already tried to make photography more than a hobby? What happened?
4. What does your partner say when you bring it up?
5. What is the number you would need to bring in before you could cut a shift a fortnight? Do you know it?
6. If someone handed you a dated plan tomorrow, what would still stop you?
7. What would it be worth to you to have your first paid shoot booked in the next 30 days?
8. If I told you a paramedic with two kids went from the road to a $300,000 a year photography business in three and a half years, what is the first thing you would think?

Question eight is the one that matters. The answer is the exact shape of their "you got lucky" objection and tells you which proof to put first.

**Kill switches**

- If fewer than six of ten say some version of "that is me" when the rung four avatar is read back to them, the rung is wrong.
- If eight founding seats cannot be filled from a warm audience with a real promise and a real guarantee, the avatar or the promise is wrong. Fix that before touching the curriculum.
