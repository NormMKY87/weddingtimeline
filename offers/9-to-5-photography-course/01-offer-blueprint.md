## 1. The brief

Facts given:

- You were a paramedic for 15 years.
- You moved to full-time photography 3.5 years ago.
- The business now runs at roughly $300,000 top-line revenue a year.
- You are a father of two and made the move with a lot riding on it.
- You have paid for many workshops, courses and business coaching programs and want to distil what actually worked.
- The product is for someone in a 9 to 5 who is dabbling in photography and wants a clear pathway to part-time or full-time creative income.

Inferred: the product is a transition and business product, not a craft product. The buyer already knows how to use a camera well enough to be paid. What they do not have is the map, the numbers, the permission and the proof that someone like them has done it.

## 2. Market check: is there a starving crowd?

Four indicators, with a verdict on each.

**Massive pain.** Yes, if you pitch the right pain. The surface want is "better photos" or "a bit of side income". The real pain is being stuck: a stable job that does not feed them, a skill people compliment them on, and no safe route between the two. The pain spikes at specific moments: a brutal shift, a birthday, a mate asking "when are you going to do this properly", a wedding they shot for free that drew more praise than their day job ever has. The pitch is the pain of staying, not the joy of photography.

**Purchasing power.** Yes, and this is the structural advantage of this avatar. They have a salary. Most photography education sells to broke photographers. You are selling to employed people who can pay. Price like a career decision, not a hobby course.

**Easy to target.** Yes. Photography Facebook groups, Instagram and YouTube photography audiences, camera store communities, workshop attendee lists, side hustle and career change audiences. The sharpest sub-group is shift workers in emergency services, healthcare and trades. They are heavily aggregated (unions, rosters, Facebook groups, station culture) and your story lands hardest with them.

**Growing.** Side income and career change interest is growing (inferred, not sourced). Weddings and portraits are a durable market. You are not creating demand, you are channelling it. Treat the pre-sale in section 13 as the real test.

Verdict: a starving crowd exists. The job is to narrow until the offer cannot be compared to anything else.

## 3. The avatar: narrow until it hurts

The niche ladder (same content, rising price and conviction):

| Level | Avatar | What it sounds like | Price ceiling (inferred) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Anyone who wants to make money from photography | "Photography business course" | $200 to $500, competes with free YouTube |
| 2 | 9 to 5 workers who want photography income | "Side hustle photography" | $500 to $1,000 |
| 3 | Salaried workers with a mortgage and kids who want a safe, staged exit into professional photography | "The exit plan" | $1,500 to $3,000 |
| 4 | Shift workers and salaried professionals, roughly 30 to 45, with a family and a mortgage, already shooting for friends for free or cheap, who want a staged plan to replace their salary with photography income without risking the household | "Someone like me actually did this" | $2,500 to $5,000, plus a one to one backend |

Recommendation: build for Level 4 and market to Level 3. Your story is an exact match for Level 4, and the family obligation is the real objection every other course ignores. A 24 year old YouTuber cannot copy this position.

### Avatar sketch: the Responsible Dabbler

Known from the brief: employed 9 to 5 (or shift roster), dabbling in photography, wants a clear pathway to part-time or full-time.

Inferred profile:

- Age roughly 30 to 45, partnered, one or more kids, mortgage or rent that depends on their salary.
- Has decent gear and is better than they think. Has shot friends, family, a mate's wedding, maybe a few $200 to $500 jobs.
- Has already tried: YouTube, a gear upgrade, possibly a $300 to $800 editing or "photography business" course, maybe a free shoot for exposure.
- Tells themselves: "I need to be better first." "I can't risk it with the kids." "People like me don't do this." "I'll do it when things settle down." "I don't know what to charge." "I'd have no idea where the clients would come from."
- Partner position: supportive in theory, nervous in practice. The partner is a silent second buyer.

### The one pain point

"I don't know how to get from where I am to a real photography income without putting my family at risk."

Everything in the offer serves that sentence. Craft is assumed. Confidence, money, time and sequence are the product.

### Genre recommendation

Anchor the pathway on weddings and portraits (people photography). Reasons: it is your proven path, it is the highest ticket entry available to a weekend operator, and it fits a 9 to 5 because the work is on weekends. Include a short module on choosing a lane so commercial, family or event shooters are not excluded, but do not dilute the examples. [NORM TO CONFIRM]

## 4. Dream outcome

In the customer's words:

"I handed in my notice on my own terms. The mortgage was covered before I did it. My partner was with me, not worried about me. I earn a proper income doing work I love, and I am home for more of my kids' lives than I ever was on shift."

Status lens (how others see them once they have it):

- Partner: responsible, not reckless. They did this properly.
- Colleagues: the one who actually did it, not the one who talked about it.
- Kids: a parent who built something.
- Other photographers: a professional with a business, not a hobbyist with a camera.

Vehicle check: this outcome could be delivered by a course, a cohort, coaching or a done-for-you setup. That confirms it is a desire, not a product.

Shorter sales line: "Replace your salary with photography, in stages, without risking your household."

## 5. Value equation read

Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay x Effort and Sacrifice)

Scores for what the avatar currently buys, from the buyer's chair. 10 is best in every row (fastest, least effort).

| Driver | Free YouTube | Generic "photography business" course | Business coach ($3k to $10k) | This offer, as designed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| Perceived likelihood | 3 (no proof it works for someone like me) | 4 (made by full-timers who never had a day job in the way) | 6 (generic, not photography, not family-constrained) | 9 (a paramedic father of two did it, and the plan is built around the day job and the kids) |
| Time delay | 2 (years of noodling) | 4 (no first-win design) | 6 | 9 (first paid booking inside 30 days, salary replacement mapped in stages) |
| Effort and sacrifice | 2 (figure it all out alone) | 4 (watch videos, then still build everything) | 5 | 9 (done-for-you templates, calculators, scripts, a 10 hour a week design) |

Weakest driver in the market: perceived likelihood, followed closely by time delay. Nobody is selling certainty to this person. Everyone is selling information.

Where this offer wins:

- Likelihood: your transition is the proof. Specificity (shift worker, kids, mortgage) multiplies it. A conditional guarantee locks it in.
- Time delay: design an early win they can feel. The first paid booking inside 30 days is the win. Then a visible countdown toward their Exit Number.
- Effort: give them the things, not the lecture about the things. Pricing done, contracts done, enquiry replies done, workflow done.

Psychological solution to borrow: the dotted train map. The Exit Number Dashboard (section 10) shows them exactly how far from the exit they are, in dollars and bookings, at all times. It does not change their income. It changes how it feels to be on the way.

### The 10x and one tenth tests

If they paid ten times the price (around $25,000), what would you provide? You would fly in or drive out and shoot their first wedding alongside them. You would do their partner call personally. You would build their whole business in a weekend with them. You would take their enquiries for 90 days. You would be on call the day they resign.

If they paid one tenth (around $250) and it still had to work, how would you deliver? A single recorded pathway, the Exit Number Calculator, the First Price Calculator, the starter contract, and the 30 day first booking plan, with a community forum instead of calls.

Both ends generate the middle. The 10x list tells you what the premium backend tier is. The one tenth list tells you what the self-paced tier is, and what the lead magnet is.

## 9. Trim and stack

Order of operations for a first offer: create flow, monetise flow, then add friction. Over-deliver on the founding cohort, learn what they actually use, then simplify.

**Cut first (high cost, low value)**
- Flying out to shoot their first wedding alongside them (keep as a paid add-on only, if ever).
- Taking their enquiries for them for 90 days.
- Daily one to one check-ins.
- Live editing their files one to one (replace with a workshop and the workflow video).

**Cut next (low cost, low value)**
- A long "history of my career" module (replace with one tight proof module).
- Generic craft lessons (not the product).
- A paid ads course (replace with a decision tree and a pointer; most of this avatar is not ready).
- Daily motivational emails.

**Keep (low cost, high value): the engine of the offer**
- Exit Number Calculator and Runway Calculator.
- First Price Calculator.
- Kitchen Table Conversation script and Partner Brief.
- Starter contract, invoice and payment templates.
- Enquiry reply templates and shift-worker auto-replies.
- Outreach scripts (friends, strangers, vendors, second shooting).
- One Weekend Setup checklist.
- Fast edit workflow video and presets.
- Shoot day playbook and wedding day timeline builder.
- Step Down Plan with decision triggers and resignation scripts.
- Progress tracker and the 30 day first booking deadline.
- Buddy pairs.

**Keep (high cost, high value), scaled to a level you can live with**
- Weekly live cohort calls for 12 weeks (one to many live, not one to one).
- Portfolio, pricing and Instagram reviews done in group, not privately.
- A monthly alumni call after the cohort.
- One to one calls reserved for the premium tier only.

One to many assets to build once: the three calculators, the timeline builder, the template vault, the recorded modules. These cost you one build and then almost nothing per student, and they are the things this avatar will pay for because they remove effort.

## 11. Enhancers

**Scarcity.** Real and justified: you are a working photographer running a $300,000 business with a family. Cap the founding cohort at 12 to 15. Say why: "I run this live and I still shoot weddings. Fifteen is the most I can give proper attention to." Later cohorts can grow, or you move to a self-paced tier plus a smaller premium cohort.

**Urgency.** Cohort start dates, not fake countdown timers. Founding price for cohort one only, rising for cohort two. The honest urgency line: "Every month you wait is another month of salary you could already be replacing."

**Bonuses (next logical need).** Present after the core stack has been anchored. Each must raise likelihood, remove an objection, cut friction or speed results.

1. The Partner Pass: the partner can attend the Kitchen Table session and the numbers night. Removes the silent second buyer's objection. ($0 cost, high value)
2. Norm's Template Vault: your actual working templates, adapted. From what already exists in your business this appears to include enquiry responses, a pricing guide and a wedding day timeline builder [NORM TO CONFIRM what you are willing to share]. Raises likelihood: "these are the ones he uses."
3. The Hot Seat: one portfolio or pricing review on a live call. Speeds the first booking.
4. The Shift Worker's Response Kit: auto-replies and the five minute system (already in the stack, pull it out and name it as a bonus if the cohort skews shift workers). Splinter stacking.
5. Alumni access for 12 months. Solves "nobody is checking in on me" and feeds the backend offer.

**Guarantee.** Recommended: a conditional guarantee with a "work with you until" consequence, not a cash refund as the primary mechanism. Fulfilment cost is moderate (your live time), so pure unconditional is risky; conditional filters and drives compliance.

## 13. Pre-sell before you build

The book's order: sell it, then fulfil it. Do not record 40 videos first.

1. Write the one-page offer: avatar, promise, the nine bundles, price, guarantee, start date.
2. Have 10 conversations with people who match the Level 4 avatar. Ask what they have tried, what they fear, what they would pay. Adjust the problem list with their words.
3. Open a founding cohort of 12 to 15 at $2,497 with a start date eight weeks out.
4. Fill it from your own audience first: Instagram, past second shooters and assistants, workshop contacts, the photographers who already DM you asking how you did it. [NORM TO CONFIRM where those conversations are already happening]
5. Deliver live, week by week, building each bundle the week before it is taught. Record everything. The recordings become the self-paced tier.
6. Track two numbers for every student: days to first paid booking, and progress toward the Exit Number. Those two numbers are the proof for cohort two.
7. Collect testimonials at the first booking, not at the end.
8. Raise the price for cohort two.
