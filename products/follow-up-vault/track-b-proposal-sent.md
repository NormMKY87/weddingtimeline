# Track B: proposal sent, no decision

You had the call and sent the proposal (Booking Pack). The couple has not said yes or no. These five messages run from day 2 to day 18. Messages B6 and B7 (revival and close the file) are in `revival-and-close.md`.

The rules from Track A hold here. Brackets are yours to fill. PERSONAL lines get one real sentence or nothing. Every email stays in the proposal thread. One ask per message.

One extra rule for this track: the couple has met you now. Write like it. Shorter, warmer, and more direct than the enquiry stage.

---

## B1. One line from the call

Day 2. Fully personalised. Email in the proposal thread, plus SMS or WhatsApp.

Go back to your recording or transcript (the Consult Call Kit covers how). Find one line they said, in their words, about what matters to them on the day. Quote it. Then show them how it shaped the proposal. If you did not record the call, use your notes. Never guess at a quote.

**Email**

Subject: Re: [your proposal email subject]

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> I've been thinking about something you said on our call.
>
> PERSONAL: [Their line, in quotation marks, then one sentence on what it changed. Example: "You said 'I don't want to be dragged away from the party for photos.' That one line is why the proposal has your portraits before sunset, so the evening is yours."]
>
> Have a read when you're both together. If anything in there isn't quite right, I'd rather change it than have you work around it.
>
> [Your first name]

**SMS**

> Hi [Partner 1], [Your first name] here. Lovely talking with you both on [Day]. I've sent a short note to go with the proposal, about something you said on the call. Have a look when you're together.

**WhatsApp**

> Hi [Partner 1], it's [Your first name]. Really enjoyed the call on [Day]. I've sent a note to go with the proposal about something you said that stuck with me. Read it when you're both free, no rush.

**Instagram DM** (only if the DM has been your main channel all the way through)

> Hi [Partner 1], loved talking with you both on [Day]. Something you said stuck with me and I've written about it in a note with the proposal. Have a look when you're together.

Skip it when: they have already opened the proposal and asked a question. Answer that question. The answer is your day 2 touch.

## B2. The unasked question

Day 5. Half personalised. Email.

Most couples sit on a proposal because of one question they have not asked. It is almost always price or availability. You heard which one on the call. Pick that paragraph and cut the other.

**Email**

Subject: Re: [your proposal email subject]

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Most couples have one question they don't ask straight away, so I'll have a go at answering the one I think is yours.
>
> PERSONAL, pick one:
>
> [Price] "If it's the number, tell me what you're weighing it against. I don't discount, but I can reshape what's included so you only pay for what matters on your day. And I'll tell you honestly if something in there isn't worth it for you."
>
> [Availability] "If it's the date, it's open as I write this. It stays open until a signed contract and retainer are in, from you or from anyone else. I'm not saying that to hurry you. I just don't want any surprises."
>
> Reply here, or call me on [Your mobile] if that's easier.
>
> [Your first name]

**SMS**

> Hi [Partner 1], [Your first name] here. I've sent a note answering the question I suspect you haven't asked yet. If I've guessed wrong, tell me the real one. Happy to talk it through on the phone too.

**WhatsApp**

> Hi [Partner 1], it's [Your first name]. I've had a go at answering the question I think you're sitting on, it's in your inbox. If I've guessed wrong, just tell me the real one. Call me any time on this number.

A note on the price paragraph: I don't discount, and the Booking Pack explains why. Credits and reshaping, never a lower number for the same work. If you do discount, cut the sentence. I'd rather you didn't.

Skip it when: they have already asked the question. Answer it directly instead, same day.

## B3. Another couple on your date

Day 8. Template, one variable: [Day of the other call]. Email, plus a short WhatsApp or SMS.

Only if it is true. You have a real enquiry or a real call booked for the same date. Never invent it, never imply it, never name the other couple. A couple who finds out you made it up will tell everyone they know, and they will be right to.

**Email**

Subject: Re: [your proposal email subject]

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> I want to be upfront about [Wedding date].
>
> I have a call on [Day of the other call] with another couple looking at the same date. Your date isn't held until a signed contract and retainer are in, from you or from them, and whoever books first secures it.
>
> I'm not telling you this to rush you. I'd hate for you to decide and then find the date gone. If you're leaning towards going ahead, tell me and I'll make the next step easy.
>
> [Your first name]

**SMS**

> Hi [Partner 1], [Your first name] here. I've just emailed about [Wedding date]. Another couple has asked about the same date and I wanted you to hear it from me first. Nothing has changed on my end, and no rush.

**WhatsApp**

> Hi [Partner 1], it's [Your first name]. Quick heads up, I've emailed you about [Wedding date]. Another couple has asked about the same date, and I'd rather you heard that from me than found out later. No pressure from me at all.

Skip it when: it is not true. Send B4 on day 8 instead and shift the rest of the track forward by four days.

## B5. Hold or release

Day 18. Template, one variable: [Wedding date]. Phone call first. Email the same day as the record.

This one is a phone call because you have spoken before, and because a voice gets a straight answer where an email gets silence. Thirty seconds. Then the email, whether you reached them or not.

**Phone script**

> Hi [Partner 1], it's [Your first name]. This isn't a sales call. I'm planning the next few months and wanted to know where you've landed on [Wedding date]. If you're going another way, that's completely fine and I'll release the date. If you're still deciding, tell me what's in the way and I'll see if I can help.

**Voicemail**

> Hi [Partner 1], it's [Your first name]. Not a sales call. I'm planning the months ahead and wanted to know where you've landed on [Wedding date]. Any answer is a good answer. I'll send a short email too, so you can reply whichever way is easiest.

**Email**

Subject: [Wedding date]: holding or releasing

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> I tried to call today. I'm planning the months ahead and need to know whether to keep [Wedding date] open for you.
>
> Three easy answers. "Yes" and I'll send the contract and retainer details today. "No" and I'll release the date, no hard feelings. "Stuck" and you tell me what's in the way.
>
> A quick line is all I need.
>
> [Your first name]

**SMS** (if the call went to voicemail)

> Hi [Partner 1], [Your first name] here. Left you a voicemail about [Wedding date]. Yes, no, or stuck, any answer is a good one. Email's in your inbox too.

**WhatsApp**

> Hi [Partner 1], it's [Your first name]. Tried calling about [Wedding date]. Yes, no, or stuck, any of the three is a good answer and I'll take it from there. No hard feelings whichever way.

Skip it when: they told you a decision date later than day 18 (a family conversation, a venue confirmation, a pay day). Call the day after that date instead.
