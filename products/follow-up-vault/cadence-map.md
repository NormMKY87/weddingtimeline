# The Follow-Up Vault: cadence map

Every follow-up between the first reply and the signed contract, by day and channel. Two tracks, seven messages each. Draft for Norm to confirm before the messages are written (days, count, channel per touch).

## How the Vault fits with the Booking Pack

The Booking Pack holds the first reply and the proposal email. The Vault starts the moment one of those goes out and nothing comes back. Day 0 on each track is the Booking Pack message. The Vault begins at day 2.

The first reply and the proposal are never repeated here. Nothing is sold twice.

## Three levels of personalisation

| Level | Which messages | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Fully personalised | Message 1 on each track | Written for this couple. One real detail from their enquiry or the consult, one question. Takes four or five minutes. Never automated |
| Half personalised | Message 2 on each track | Template body with a personal opening line. Their date, their venue, or the question they have not asked yet. Two minutes |
| Template, one variable | Messages 3 to 7 on each track | Ready to send. Change the one thing in [square brackets] and press send. Under a minute. Safe to automate from a CRM |

## Channel rules

- Email is the record. Every touch has an email version, kept in the same thread as the Booking Pack message so the whole conversation sits in one place.
- SMS, Instagram DM and WhatsApp go where the couple came from. If they enquired through Instagram, the DM is the main channel and the email is the backup. If they gave a mobile and you have texted before, SMS or WhatsApp sits alongside the email.
- A second channel on the same day points at the first. It never carries a second ask.
- One ask per touch. One link at most.
- A phone call replaces the message where the map says so. Leave a voicemail, then send the email version the same day as the record.

## Timing rules

- Count from the send date of the Booking Pack message (day 0), not from when they read it.
- Any reply from the couple stops the track. From then on it is a conversation, not a sequence. If they go quiet again later, restart from the next unsent touch rather than from the top.
- A date you promised wins. If you said "I'll check in Thursday", Thursday beats the map.
- If a touch lands on a weekend or a public holiday, send it the next weekday morning.
- The map assumes couples booking six to eighteen months out. For short-notice weddings (under four months), halve every gap.

## Where the tracks can grow

Norm's starting count is six to seven per track and he intends to add more. These are the slots that take an extra touch without crowding the rest:

- Track A, day 28: a second value touch (a different resource) between the soft close and the revival.
- Track B, day 24: a short "anything I can change" note between the hold or release call and the revival.
- Either track, within 48 hours of a real event: a venue you both know posts something, a date near theirs gets booked, a planner they named mentions you. Real triggers beat calendar triggers every time.
