How the Vault works

The Booking Pack holds your first reply and your proposal email. The Vault starts the moment one of those goes out and nothing comes back. Day 0 is the Booking Pack message. The Vault begins at day 2.

Fully personalised

Message 1 on each track. Written for this couple. One real detail from their enquiry or the consult, one question. Four or five minutes. Never automated.

Half personalised

Message 2 on each track. Template body with a personal opening line. Their date, their venue, or the question they have not asked yet. Two minutes.

Template, one variable

Messages 3 to 7. Ready to send. Change the one thing in [square brackets] and press send. Under a minute. Safe to automate from a CRM.

Channel rules

  • Email is the record. Every touch has an email version, kept in the same thread as the Booking Pack message.
  • SMS, Instagram DM and WhatsApp go where the couple came from. Enquired through Instagram, the DM leads and the email backs it up.
  • A second channel on the same day points at the first. It never carries a second ask.
  • One ask per touch. One link at most.
  • A phone call replaces the message where the map says so. Voicemail, then the email the same day as the record.

Timing rules

  • Count from the send date of the Booking Pack message, not from when they read it.
  • Any reply stops the track. From then on it is a conversation, not a sequence. If they go quiet again, restart from the next unsent touch.
  • A date you promised wins. "I'll check in Thursday" beats the map.
  • A touch that lands on a weekend or a public holiday goes out the next weekday morning.
  • Built for couples booking six to eighteen months out. Under four months, halve every gap.

Reading the messages

  • Anything in [square brackets] is yours to fill. Delete the brackets.
  • Lines marked PERSONAL get one real sentence about this couple, or nothing. Never a generic line.
  • Sign off with your first name. Your signature does the rest.
  • Under every message: when to skip it. Read that line before you send.

Ghosted lead revival and closing the file

The last two messages on each track

The revival brings a quiet lead back with something true. The close-the-file message ends the sequence kindly, releases the date, and leaves the door open.

Rules for revival

  • Point to something true. A wedding you shot at a venue like theirs. A resource you just finished. If none of it is true, do not send it. Wait until something is, up to ten days past the mapped day.
  • Never manufacture scarcity. "Dates are filling fast" is a lie unless you can name the dates.
  • Never count the unanswered messages. It is a guilt trip, and it does not work.
  • Never ask "are you still interested". It invites a no and sounds like everyone else.
  • One revival per track. If it does not land, you close the file.

Rules for closing the file

  • Always send it. This is the one touch that never gets skipped.
  • Release the date the same day, in your calendar and your CRM. Lost reason: no response.
  • Offer to keep in touch only if they say yes. "Yes please" is the only way onto your nurture list.
  • Then stop. A couple who comes back after this does so because you stopped, not despite it.

After the file is closed

  1. Mark the enquiry lost in your CRM the same day. Reason: no response, or date taken.
  2. Release the date in every calendar you check availability against.
  3. Delete every remaining to-do for this couple.
  4. If they replied "yes please", add them to your keep-in-touch list. Nobody else goes on it.
  5. If they come back later, they start a fresh conversation, not a sequence. Reply like a person, book the call, and let the Booking Pack take it from there.
The to-do rule

If it has no to-do, it is not a lead

A follow-up that lives in your head is a hope. The rule fixes that in one move: the moment you send anything to a couple, set the to-do for the next touch before you close the tab.

Not after the next enquiry. Not at the end of the day. Before you close the tab. The default gap is two days. The cadence map gives the exact day for each touch, and the to-do carries three things: the couple's names, the message code, and the channel.

Example to-do

Fri 14 Mar. A3 email + SMS. Priya and Tom.

Why two days

Long enough that you are not pestering, short enough that they have not forgotten you, and short enough that a to-do can slip a day and still land inside the window. Every other gap in the map is built outward from that first two-day step.

The four moves

1

Reply, then set

First reply goes out, the A1 to-do goes in for two days from today. Same for the proposal: send it, set B1. One minute, every time.

2

They reply, you reset

Any reply kills every open to-do on their track. Delete them. Set one new to-do for whatever you promised in your reply. The conversation sets the dates now.

3

They go quiet again, you resume

Pick the track up at the next unsent touch, counting from your last message. Do not start from A1 again.

4

Close the file, clear the list

The last message on each track releases the date and ends the to-dos. Nothing for that couple stays on your list.

In a CRM

Any CRM works. The steps are the same.

  1. Open the couple's record straight after you send.
  2. Add a task. Title: day, message code, channel, names. Due date from the map.
  3. If your CRM runs workflows, build one per track that creates all seven to-dos when the couple moves into "Replied" or "Proposal sent". Your only manual job is deleting them when the couple replies.
  4. Have the CRM email you the day's to-dos each morning. Do them in one sitting.

In a calendar

No CRM yet? Your phone calendar is enough.

  1. The day you send the first reply, create one all-day event per touch, seven in total, titled "A1 email + SMS: Priya and Tom" and so on, on the days from the map.
  2. One colour that means "follow-up" and nothing else.
  3. When the couple replies, search their names, select all, delete.
  4. A to-do on a weekend or holiday gets dragged to the next weekday morning.

Paper works too. The system matters less than the rule.

Batching and the weekly sweep

Set one window a day for follow-ups. Twenty minutes, same time every day, before your shift or after it. Open the list, send everything due, set the next to-dos, close the list. Nothing from this product gets sent at nine at night from the couch unless nine at night is your window.

Friday, ten minutes. Open every enquiry and every proposal that is not booked and not closed. Each one has a to-do with a date on it, or you set one now. An open lead with no to-do is the only failure this system has, and the sweep catches it.

If it has no to-do, it is not a lead.
