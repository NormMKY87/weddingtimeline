# The to-do rule

A follow-up that lives in your head is not a follow-up. It is a hope. The rule fixes that in one move.

## The rule

The moment you send anything to a couple, set the to-do for the next touch before you close the tab. Not after the next enquiry. Not at the end of the day. Before you close the tab.

The default gap is two days. The cadence map tells you the exact day for each touch, and the to-do carries three things: the couple's names, the message code (A1, B3), and the channel.

Example: "Fri 14 Mar. A3 email + SMS. Priya and Tom."

## Why two days

Two days is long enough that you are not pestering and short enough that they have not forgotten you. It is also short enough that a to-do can slip a day and still land inside the window. Every other gap in the map is built outward from that first two-day step.

## The four moves

**1. Reply, then set.** First reply goes out. The A1 to-do goes in for two days from today. Same for the proposal: send it, set B1 for two days out. One minute, every time, no exceptions.

**2. They reply, you reset.** Any reply from the couple kills every open to-do on their track. Delete them. Then set one new to-do for whatever you promised in your reply ("I'll check in Thursday" means a Thursday to-do). You are in a conversation now, and the conversation sets the dates.

**3. They go quiet again, you resume.** If the conversation stalls, pick the track up at the next unsent touch, counting from your last message. Do not start from A1 again.

**4. Close the file, clear the list.** The last message on each track releases the date and ends the to-dos. Nothing for that couple stays on your list.

## Setting it in a CRM

Any CRM works. Dubsado, HubSpot, Studio Ninja, HoneyBook, Táve, a Notion board. The steps are the same:

1. Open the couple's record straight after you send.
2. Add a task or to-do. Title: day, message code, channel, names. Due date: from the cadence map.
3. If your CRM runs workflows, build one per track that creates all seven to-dos the moment you move the couple into "Replied" or "Proposal sent". Then your only manual job is deleting them when the couple replies.
4. Set the CRM to email you the day's to-dos each morning. Do them in one sitting.

## Setting it in a calendar

If you have no CRM yet, your phone calendar is enough:

1. The day you send the first reply, create one all-day event per touch. Seven events, titled "A1 email + SMS: Priya and Tom", "A2 email: Priya and Tom", and so on, on the days from the map.
2. Give them one colour that means "follow-up" and nothing else.
3. When the couple replies, delete the remaining events in one go. Search their names, select all, delete.
4. When a to-do falls on a weekend or a holiday, drag it to the next weekday morning.

Paper works too. A page per week, the couple's name on the day, crossed off when sent. The system matters less than the rule.

## Batching

Set one window a day for follow-ups. Twenty minutes, same time every day, before your shift or after it. Open the list, send everything due, set the next to-dos, close the list. Nothing from this product gets sent at 9pm from the couch unless 9pm is your window.

## The weekly sweep

Friday, ten minutes. Open every enquiry and every proposal that is not booked and not closed. Each one has a to-do with a date on it, or you set one now. An open lead with no to-do is the only failure this system has, and the sweep catches it.

## The one line to remember

If it has no to-do, it is not a lead.
