# Quit-Number Calculator: copy

All copy in first person singular, Australian English, no dashes, no semicolons. The page copy lives in `app/index.html` and is reproduced here so it can be reviewed and reused in the free channel. No prices anywhere. No email capture (Norm, 9 November 2026).

## Page

Eyebrow: A tool from Norman Yap, for photographers with a day job

Headline: Can I leave my job, and when?

Lede: I was a paramedic shooting weddings on the side. This is the maths I wish I had run before I handed in my notice. It compares your take-home pay to what the business must actually earn, builds in a buffer, and gives you a date. Twenty minutes, your real numbers, no guessing. It works in any country, because every rate comes from your own payslip and your own tax office.

Who it is for: For wedding photographers who are already booking paid weddings. If you are not earning from weddings yet, follow along and come back when you are.

Tabs: The calculator. The Package Ladder.

Steps: Step 1. Your job. Step 2. Your business. Step 3. Your targets and the path. Then your number.

## Where you work (step 1)

Australia: Australian income tax, Medicare levy and super guarantee for 2026-27 are built in and sourced, so I can estimate your take-home from your salary. You can still type your real take-home from a payslip.

Somewhere else: I do not carry tax tables for other countries, and I will not guess them. You type what your payslip and your tax office already tell you: gross pay, take-home, the tax rate on your next dollar, your employer's retirement contribution and your sales tax rate. Everything else works the same.

Button at the end of step 3: Show me my number

## Result page

Headline: Your quit number. [X] a month.

Sub: [$Y] a year of business profit, after every cost of running it and before your own tax. It lands [$Z] a month in your bank, the same as the job does now.

Tiles: Gap a month. Business profit this year, before tax. Overhead per wedding at [n] a year. Net margin today.

The paragraph in my voice changes with the result:

- Gap closed: On paper the business already pays for the job. I would not resign on paper. Your question now is the buffer and the calendar: [buffer] in the household account before you go, and enough signed contracts in the next twelve months that the quit number is covered by work already booked. Run the Package Ladder anyway, because a thin margin at this volume breaks the first quiet season.
- Price below cost: At your current average sale each wedding costs you more than it brings in once card fees and direct costs come out. Nothing else on this page matters until the price moves. Open the Package Ladder and price the floor tier first.
- Not growing: You booked the same or fewer weddings this year than last, so at this trajectory the dates never arrive. That is useful to know now. To be at your number in 24 months you need to add about [g] bookings a year at [average], or lift the average. [n] weddings at [A] is the hard way. [n2] at [A2] is the same money with [n minus n2] fewer Saturdays. Build the ladder before you build the calendar.
- Thirty or more weddings needed: At [A] you need [n] weddings a year. That is a full-time studio's workload at a part-timer's price, and you would arrive exhausted. The lever is the average sale, not the volume. [n2] weddings at [A2] replaces the same package with [n minus n2] fewer Saturdays and more room for the quiet months. Twenty weddings at a higher average beats forty at a lower one. Open the Package Ladder next and build the prices backwards from this number.
- Otherwise: You need [n] weddings a year at [A], or [n2] at [A2]. Fund the buffer first, trial the full-time week on unpaid leave in [month year], and hold the resignation until the trial has run and the calendar for the next twelve months carries the number. The Package Ladder tab turns the number into prices.

Deposit warning: Deposits are not your buffer. The last column is other people's money sitting in your account for weddings you have not photographed. Cash at bank must be at least that before any of it counts as yours.

Scenario cards: Part time to full time. Drop to part time first. Casual bridge.

Footer: Australian rates as at 8 November 2026, financial year 2026-27, reviewed every July. Elsewhere, every rate is yours from your payslip and your tax office. This is a planning tool and not tax or financial advice. Your accountant confirms before anything is set. Your inputs stay in your browser and are never sent anywhere.
