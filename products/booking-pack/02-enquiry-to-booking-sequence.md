# Part 2. Enquiry-to-Booking sequence

The emails and texts from the moment an enquiry lands to the moment a call is booked, plus the three habits that stop couples ghosting you.

The first reply has one job. Book the call. Not explain your packages, not send a guide, not quote a price. Every line in it either answers the question they asked or moves them one step closer to a 15 minute conversation. Once I understood that, my first reply got shorter and my bookings went up.

## The five rules

1. **Answer the date first.** Couples want to know if you are free. Say it in the first two lines.
2. **No price list in the first reply.** Ask a question or two instead. You cannot quote well when you are guessing what they need. If they asked for a price directly, give a starting point and then invite the call. Never dodge.
3. **One next step per email.** One link, one ask. Never two.
4. **Close the loop.** Every enquiry ends as booked, not right for us, or released. Nothing sits in limbo.
5. **You press send on the personal ones.** Automate the acknowledgement and the reminders. Write the first reply and the proposal yourself, with these as the base.

## The three-calendar date check

Before you tell any couple you are free, check three places:

1. Your booking system or CRM, where contracts and retainers live.
2. Your personal calendar, where family, holidays and the things you will not move live.
3. The third place dates hide for you. A shared family calendar, a second shooter's roster, a venue coordinator role, a day job shift pattern.

Only when all three are clear do you write "Good news first, I'm free on [date]." If one is blocked, the date is taken. If one is unclear, say you are checking and come back the same day. Taking back a "yes" is worse than a slow "let me check".

Write the three places down and tape them above your desk. Then it is a habit, not a memory test.

## The two day follow-up to-do

The moment you send a first reply, set a to-do for two days later: "Follow up [names] if no reply." In your CRM as an automated task, in your phone as a reminder, on paper if that is what you use. The to-do is set before you move to the next email. Not later, not when you remember.

Most ghosting is the photographer's. The couple replied in their head, got busy, and nobody nudged them. The to-do is the fix. What you say on day two, five and twelve is in the Follow-Up Vault. This pack makes sure the day arrives.

## The shape

| Stage | What goes out | When | Sent by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enquiry lands | Instant text, no link, no price | Straight away, any hour | Automation |
| First reply | Email, date free, date taken, or asked for price | Same day if you can, next business day at worst | You |
| Two day to-do set | Nothing sent yet | The moment you reply | You |
| Call booked | One confirmation email, reminder the day before | Instantly, then the day before | Automation |
| After the call | The proposal email (Part 3) | Same day, while it is fresh | You |
| Booked | Welcome email | When contract and retainer are both in | Automation, checked by you |

Any reply from the couple stops every automation. From then on it is a conversation, not a sequence.

## Placeholders

| Placeholder | What goes in |
|---|---|
| [Partner 1] [Partner 2] | First names. Use both in the greeting |
| [Wedding date] | Weekday plus full date, for example "Saturday 14 March 2028" |
| [Venue] | The ceremony or reception venue they named |
| [Call link] | Your booking link for the 15 minute chat |
| [Planning resource] | One free, useful thing you give couples: a timeline planner, a light guide, a family photo list |
| [Referral photographer] | A colleague you trust for dates you cannot take |
| [Retainer terms] | Your booking terms, for example "a signed contract and a 50% retainer" |
| [Starting figure] | Your lowest starting price, used only when they ask directly |

Keep paragraphs to one to three sentences. Most of these emails sit between 80 and 200 words. No emoji in email. One exclamation mark is plenty. Sign off with your normal signature and do not type your name twice.

Lines marked PERSONAL are where you write one real sentence about this couple. Never leave a generic line there. If you have nothing real, cut the line.

## The instant text (automation)

> Hi [Partner 1], it's [your first name] from [business name]. Thanks so much for your enquiry, and congratulations! I'll reply properly by email shortly.

No link. No price. It tells them a real person saw it.

## First reply: your date is free (you send)

Subject options:
- Congratulations [Partner 1] and [Partner 2]! Your [Venue] wedding
- Your [Month Year] wedding date
- [Partner 1] and [Partner 2], good news on your date

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Congratulations to you both, and thank you so much for reaching out.
>
> Good news first. I'm free on [Wedding date], so your date is open with me.
>
> PERSONAL: [One line that shows you read their enquiry. Their venue, how they met, something they said they care about.]
>
> I'd love to give you all the information on how I work and what's included. Before I do, I'd like to find out a little more about what you have planned, so I'm not guessing or suggesting things you don't need.
>
> Can I ask: [one easy question, for example "What's the part of the day you're most looking forward to?"]
>
> If it's easier to talk it through, you can grab a time for a quick 15 minute chat here: [Call link]
>
> No rush at all.

Two questions is the most. One is better.

## First reply: your date is taken (you send)

Subject: About your [Wedding date] wedding

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Congratulations, and thank you so much for thinking of me.
>
> I wanted to let you know straight away. Unfortunately I'm already committed on [Wedding date], which is a real shame, because [one honest line about why their plans suit how you shoot].
>
> If your date has any flexibility, these dates are still open:
> - [Open date 1]
> - [Open date 2]
> - [Open date 3]
>
> If [Wedding date] is locked in, I'd recommend [Referral photographer]. [One line on why you trust them.] If her work feels right, reply with your best email and I'll introduce you both.
>
> Wishing you both all the best with the planning.

Only list open dates if they are real and close to theirs. Only refer people you would book yourself. If you do introduce them, send the introduction that night and copy your colleague in.

## First reply: they asked for prices straight away (you send)

Subject: Your [Month Year] wedding, and a straight answer on pricing

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Congratulations, and thank you for getting in touch.
>
> Good news first. I'm free on [Wedding date].
>
> You asked about pricing, so here's a straight answer. My weddings start from [Starting figure]. Where you land depends on the shape of your day, and that's the bit I don't want to guess.
>
> The quickest way to get you a real number is a 15 minute chat. I'll ask a few questions about your day and then send a proposal built around what you actually need, nothing more and nothing less.
>
> You can grab a time here: [Call link]

One starting figure only. Never a full price list in an email. If you would rather not quote a figure at all, use the first version and say pricing follows the chat.

## When another couple wants the same date

This is the paragraph I use when two couples are looking at one date. It goes in the first reply if it is already true, and in the proposal email after the call (Part 3) if it becomes true. Three rules: only when it is true, say it on the call before you write it, and never name the other couple.

> I also want to be upfront about your date. It isn't held until a signed contract and retainer are in, and I have a chat [on Thursday] with another couple looking at [Wedding date]. Whoever books first will secure it. I'd hate for you to miss out, so if you're leaning towards going ahead, let me know and I'll make it easy.

It is a fact, said once, calmly. It is not a pressure line, and it stops being true the day the other couple decides. Update it or remove it the same day.

## Call booked: confirmation (automation)

Most booking tools send a reminder on their own, so one confirmation is enough. If yours does not, send the same email again the day before with the subject "Speak tomorrow".

Subject: You're booked in, [Partner 1] and [Partner 2]

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Lovely, you're locked in for [Call date and time] ([time zone]).
>
> Here's the link to join: [Meeting link]
>
> It's a relaxed chat. I'll ask about your day, what you're picturing and anything you're unsure about. Bring any questions you have. If you can both be on the call, even better.
>
> One thing to think about beforehand, if you have a minute: [one easy prompt, for example "the moment of the day you most want to remember"].
>
> If something comes up and you need to move it, you can change the time here: [Reschedule link]
>
> Speak soon.

## Booked: welcome (automation, checked by you)

Trigger only when the contract is signed and the retainer has landed. A verbal yes is not booked. A signed contract on its own is not booked.

Subject: You're booked! Welcome, [Partner 1] and [Partner 2]

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> It's official. Your contract and retainer are in, and [Wedding date] is locked in with me. I'm so excited to be part of it.
>
> Here's what happens from here:
> - [Step 1, for example "You'll get your receipt and a copy of your signed contract by email."]
> - [Step 2, for example "Around three months before the wedding, we'll plan the timeline together."]
> - [Step 3, for example "The week of the wedding, I'll send a final check-in."]
>
> You don't need to do anything else right now. Enjoy being booked in.
>
> If anything changes, or a question pops into your head at 11pm, just reply here.
>
> Can't wait to celebrate with you both.

Sell certainty here: tell them when they will hear from you next. If you include a pre-wedding session, add one line on how to book it.

## Pipeline stages to set up

1. New enquiry
2. Replied (first reply sent, two day to-do set)
3. Call booked
4. Proposal sent
5. Booked (won)
6. Not proceeding (lost, with a reason)
7. Nurture (permission given to keep in touch)

Lost reasons worth tracking: date taken, budget, chose someone else, no response, wedding postponed, not the right fit. After three months of tracking these you will know exactly which part of your process to fix next.

## Words that work, and words to drop

**Use:** good news first, quick chat, no rush at all, so I'm not guessing, nothing more and nothing less, close the loop, release the date, completely fine, a quick line either way.

**Drop:** just checking in, touching base, per my last email, kindly, don't hesitate to contact, I hope this email finds you well, limited spots, act now, your special day, are you still interested?
