# Part 3. The proposal

A proposal is a note from you, not a quote.

The couple just spent 15 minutes telling you about their day. The proposal proves you listened. It is written in the first person, it recaps what they said in their words, it lists what they get as dot points, and it gives exact dates for when they will see their photos. The price sits inside that, once, plainly. If a couple can read your proposal and not tell it was written for them, it is a quote, and quotes get compared.

I send the proposal the same day as the call, while it is fresh for both of us. Never before a call.

## The rules

- **First person singular.** "I'll be with you from the getting ready." Never "our team will provide coverage."
- **Their words, not yours.** If they said "chill", write chill. Do not translate it into "relaxed and organic".
- **Deliverables as dot points.** Short, concrete, in the order of the day. No paragraphs of inclusions.
- **Exact gallery dates.** Not "six to eight weeks". A calendar date you can beat. Work it out from your real turnaround plus a buffer, then write the date down. A date is a promise a couple can hold. A range is a hedge they can feel.
- **One link.** The proposal page or document, if you use one. The email carries the recap, the page carries the detail.
- **Say what booked means.** Signed contract plus retainer received. Not a verbal yes. Say it once, calmly.
- **Read it on your phone before you send it.** No square brackets left anywhere. If you are unsure of a detail, write it more generally and ask the question in the email.

## The proposal email

This is the proposal for most couples. If you use a proposal page or a document in your CRM, the email still carries everything below and the page holds the longer version.

Subject options:
- Thank you! Next steps for [Partner 1] and [Partner 2]
- Your wedding proposal and next steps

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Thank you for taking the time to chat with me [this afternoon]. It was lovely getting to know you both.
>
> PERSONAL: [Name something specific from the call in their words. A tradition, a person, a worry they had. Then their venue, so it reads as written for them. For example: "I loved hearing how much the ceremony with your grandparents there means to you, and I'm already picturing the light at [Venue] around five."]
>
> **What I'd suggest for your day**
>
> - [The shape of the coverage in plain words, as a moment, for example "With you from the getting ready through to the first dance"]
> - [Who is there, for example "Me as your photographer for the whole day" or "Me plus a second photographer for the ceremony and the start of the reception"]
> - [Anything you changed for them on the call]
> - [Anything you promised on the call]
> - [What they receive, for example "Your full gallery, edited, in an online gallery you can download from and share"]
> - [Any product included, for example "A pre-wedding session so you're used to me and the camera before the day"]
>
> **When you'll see your photos**
>
> - A first look of [number] photos by [exact date, for example Tuesday 17 March 2028]
> - Your full gallery by [exact date, for example Friday 8 May 2028]
>
> **The investment**
>
> [Price, once, inc GST if that applies to you.] To lock in [Wedding date], it's [Retainer terms]. Your date is confirmed once the signed contract and retainer are both in. If spreading the balance into instalments suits you better, just say so and I'll set that up.
>
> [If a proposal page or document exists:] Here it is in full, with the contract and payment steps: **[Wedding proposal]** (hyperlinked)
>
> **Next steps**
>
> 1. Have a read through together.
> 2. Reply with any questions, or anything you'd like changed. I'd rather you only pay for what you need.
> 3. When you're ready, sign and pay the retainer and the date is yours.
>
> If it's okay with you, I'll check in on [specific day] to see how you're both feeling.

Then your normal signature.

### The competing enquiry paragraph

Only when it is true, only after you have said it on the call, never naming the other couple. It sits directly under the investment paragraph.

> I also want to be upfront about your date. It isn't held until a signed contract and retainer are in, and I have a chat [on Thursday] with another couple looking at [Wedding date]. Whoever books first will secure it. I'd hate for you to miss out, so if you're leaning towards going ahead, let me know and I'll make it easy.

## Worked example

Here is the same email filled in for an imagined couple, so you can see the length and the feel. Every detail is invented.

> Hi Priya and Tom,
>
> Thank you for taking the time to chat with me this afternoon. It was lovely getting to know you both.
>
> I loved hearing that the bit you're most nervous about is the family photos, and the bit you'll treasure most is the ceremony with Tom's grandmother there. The garden at your venue faces west, so your portraits will land in the softest light of the day.
>
> **What I'd suggest for your day**
>
> - With you from the getting ready at the house through to the first dance
> - Me as your photographer for the whole day
> - A family photo list we build together three weeks out, so the family photos take 20 minutes, not an hour
> - A sunset portrait window of 25 minutes, planned into your timeline
> - Your full gallery, edited, in an online gallery you can download from and share
> - A relaxed pre-wedding session so you're used to me and the camera before the day
>
> **When you'll see your photos**
>
> - A first look of 30 photos by Tuesday 17 March 2028
> - Your full gallery by Friday 8 May 2028
>
> **The investment**
>
> [Price] inc GST. To lock in Saturday 14 March 2028, it's a signed contract and a 50% retainer. Your date is confirmed once both are in. If spreading the balance into instalments suits you better, just say so and I'll set that up.
>
> **Next steps**
>
> 1. Have a read through together.
> 2. Reply with any questions, or anything you'd like changed. I'd rather you only pay for what you need.
> 3. When you're ready, sign and pay the retainer and the date is yours.
>
> If it's okay with you, I'll check in on Thursday to see how you're both feeling.

Around 250 words. It took five minutes to write off the notes from the call, and almost all of that time went on the second paragraph.

## If you use a proposal page

Some CRMs let you send a designed page with the contract and payment attached. If yours does, build it once and change only the personal blocks per couple. The order follows the couple's thinking: do I like you, do I trust how you work, what does it cost, what is the catch, what do I do now.

| Block | Job | Changes per couple |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hero | Their names, their date, their venue, one photo | Yes |
| 2. Letter and recap | The proposal email above, as the opening | Yes |
| 3. How I work | Three beliefs, each answering a worry you hear on calls | No |
| 4. From here to your gallery | Six steps from booking to gallery, with your exact gallery dates in the last one | Dates only |
| 5. Your options | Two to four collections as cards, same inclusions in the same order, price once per card | Only if you tailored one |
| 6. Add to your day | Three to five add-ons, each with one line on when it is worth it | No |
| 7. Questions couples ask | Six to eight real questions from your inbox, answered in two or three sentences | No |
| 8. Ready to book | Choose, sign, pay. The booked rule once. One button | No |
| 9. Your details | Only the fields you will use. Prefill what you already have | No |

Notes on the blocks that cause trouble:

- **Hero.** Their names first, your logo small. No "proposal prepared for". Their names already say it.
- **How I work.** If you cannot picture a couple saying "yes, that's what I want", it is a slogan, not a belief. Cut it. Mine are about planning the day around the light, directing gently then letting moments happen, and finishing the plan weeks before the day. Yours will be different.
- **Your options.** Describe coverage by the moments it covers, not by hours. Couples picture their day in moments. Hours make them start counting. Name the collections after what they give, never bronze, silver and gold. If you tailored one on the call, mark it "Suggested for you" and put it first. No crossed-out prices, no "limited time".
- **Add-ons.** "Worth it if you have over 120 guests or two getting-ready locations" helps a couple decide. "Second photographer" on its own does not.
- **Questions.** Keep a list of what couples ask on calls for a month and you will have it. Every answer must match your contract. If they disagree, the couple trusts the contract and stops trusting you.
- **Test it as a couple.** Fake names, choose a collection, sign, reach the payment screen. Then do it again on your phone. Check the greeting with one name filled in and with two. "Dear and ," is the first thing a couple sees if your merge fields fail.

Whatever tool you use, three things link in order: the proposal, the contract, the first payment. If the couple has to leave the page to find any of them, you will lose some of them in the gap.
