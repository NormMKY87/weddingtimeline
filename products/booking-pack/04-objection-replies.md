# Part 4. Objection replies

The five things couples say when they are close to booking and not quite there, and what I say back. Each one has an email version and a DM version. The email is longer because email can hold a thought. The DM is two bubbles because that is what a DM can hold.

Before the scripts, the stance that sits under all of them.

## Where I stand on discounting

I do not discount. Not for a Sunday, not for a short day, not for a couple I really like, not to beat another quote. Here is why, and it has nothing to do with pride.

A discount teaches the couple that the first price was never real. Every price you quote after that is a starting point for a negotiation. The couples who booked you at full price hear about it, because couples talk. And the couple who got the discount now wonders what else was padded.

A gifted product does the same job in disguise. Throwing in the album to close the booking tells them the album was worth nothing to you. If you sell albums, you have just made every future album harder to sell.

So the rule is simple. Never cut the price. Never give away a product to close. If something has to move, move the scope or offer a credit.

- **Move the scope.** Fewer hours of coverage, no second photographer, the pre-wedding session taken out. The price falls because they are buying less, and every line still costs what it costs.
- **Offer a credit.** If you want to do something generous, make it a credit toward something they would buy later: prints, an album upgrade, an anniversary session. It costs you less than a discount, it lands after the wedding when they love you most, and it never touches the price of the wedding itself.

Spreading payments is not a discount. If the total is right and the timing is the problem, change the timing. Instalments across the months before the wedding are fine. A tiny first payment is not, because it invites an easy yes and an easy cancel. Keep the retainer meaningful and spread the balance.

One more thing. Say your price once, plainly, and then stop talking about it. The urge to justify a number is what makes it sound negotiable.

## 1. Price pushback

"That's more than we expected." "It's a bit out of our range." "Can you do anything on the price?"

Hold the price, acknowledge the reaction, and offer to change the shape of the day instead.

**Email**

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Thank you for being straight with me. It's a lot of money, and I'd rather you said so than went quiet.
>
> I don't discount, and I'll tell you why. Every couple I work with pays the same for the same thing, and that's the only way I can look any of them in the eye. What I can do is change the shape of what you're buying.
>
> A few ways that could look:
> - [Fewer hours, for example "Start at the ceremony instead of the getting ready, which brings the coverage down to [X] hours"]
> - [Remove an inclusion, for example "Take out the second photographer. With [guest count] guests and one location I can cover it well on my own"]
> - [Move something later, for example "Leave the album out for now and decide after you've seen the gallery"]
>
> If one of those feels right, tell me and I'll send a revised proposal tonight. And if the numbers just don't work, that's completely fine too. I'd rather you had the right photographer than a stretched budget.
>
> Looking forward to hearing your thoughts.

**DM**

> **Couple:** It's a bit more than we'd budgeted for. Is there anything you can do on the price?
>
> **Me:** Totally fair, and thanks for saying it rather than going quiet.
>
> **Me:** I don't discount, but I can change the shape of the day. Would it help to look at a shorter coverage, or taking the second photographer out?
>
> **Why:** The position in one line, then the shape of the day as the thing that moves. Never the price.

## 2. The budget question

"What's your budget?" is a question I never ask on a call. I listen for it instead. But when a couple raises budget themselves, before or after the proposal, I treat it as the best thing they could have said.

"We're working to a budget of around [figure]." "Is there anything you can do for [figure]?" "We only have [figure] for photography."

**Email, before the proposal**

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Thank you for telling me. Budget is the conversation a lot of my favourite couples have with me before they book, so I'm glad you raised it.
>
> Can I ask one thing before I put anything together? What matters most to you in the photos? The whole day told start to finish, the portraits, the family, the party. If I know that, I can shape coverage that fits [figure] and spends it on the right parts of the day, rather than guessing.
>
> Every wedding I shoot is built this way. The gap between what you have and what you need is often smaller than it looks on paper.
>
> Looking forward to hearing your thoughts.

**Email, after the proposal**

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Thanks for letting me know where you've landed. [Figure] is a real number and I can work with it.
>
> The proposal I sent covers [the shape of the day]. To bring it to [figure], here's what I'd change:
> - [The change, for example "Finish after the first dance rather than staying through to the end of the night"]
> - [The change, for example "Leave the pre-wedding session out"]
>
> That keeps [what matters most to them, in their words] exactly as we talked about. The parts I'd take out are the parts you said mattered least.
>
> If that works, I'll send the revised proposal through tonight.
>
> Looking forward to hearing your thoughts.

**DM**

> **Couple:** We're working to about [figure] for photography.
>
> **Me:** Thanks for telling me, that actually makes it easier.
>
> **Me:** What matters most to you in the photos? If I know that I can shape the coverage to fit, rather than guess.
>
> **Why:** Asking what matters most turns a number into a brief I can build to.

What I never do here: drop the price to meet the figure and keep the same scope. That is a discount wearing a budget costume, and the couple knows it.

## 3. Still deciding

"We're still deciding." "We're talking to a couple of other photographers." "We need a bit more time."

Give them the time, make it easy to come back, and be honest about the date.

**Email**

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Of course. It's a big decision and you should take the time you need.
>
> Two things that might help. First, if there's anything in the proposal you'd like changed, or anything you're not sure about, ask me. I'd rather fix it than have it sit there as a reason not to book. Second, [Wedding date] is still open with me as of today, but I don't hold dates without a booking, so if another couple asks about it I'll let you know straight away.
>
> Is there a day that suits you for me to check in? I'll put it in my diary and leave you alone until then.
>
> Looking forward to hearing your thoughts.

**DM**

> **Couple:** We're still deciding, we're talking to a couple of others as well.
>
> **Me:** Of course, no rush at all. It's a big call.
>
> **Me:** Is there anything in the proposal you'd like changed, or a day that suits for me to check in?
>
> **Why:** Two ways to come back, one message. If they name a day, that day wins.

If they name a day, that day wins over every follow-up in your system. If they do not, the Follow-Up Vault takes over from here.

## 4. Partner wants someone else

"My partner likes another photographer's style." "We can't agree." "My fiancé wants to go with someone a friend used."

Do not argue with the partner you have not met. Get them both on a call, or get out of the way gracefully.

**Email**

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Thank you for being honest with me. This happens more than you'd think, and it's usually because one of you has had a 15 minute conversation with me and the other has only seen a website.
>
> If [Partner 2] is open to it, I'd love ten minutes with you both on a call. Not to talk anyone out of anything. Just so the decision is made with the same information on both sides. If after that the other photographer is the right fit, I'll be the first to say so.
>
> Would [two times] suit for a quick chat with you both?
>
> Looking forward to hearing your thoughts.

**DM**

> **Couple:** I love your work but [Partner 2] is leaning towards another photographer.
>
> **Me:** Thanks for telling me, honestly. That's usually because one of you has chatted with me and the other's only seen the website.
>
> **Me:** Would [Partner 2] be up for a quick ten minute chat with the three of us? No pressure, just so you're deciding on the same info.
>
> **Why:** Ten minutes with both of them is the only thing that changes this. Framed as fairness, it is not pushy.

If they say no to the call, close warmly and mean it. A couple who chooses someone else and feels respected sends you their friends.

> Completely understand. You should both love your photographer, and if that's [other photographer], you've chosen well. Have the most wonderful day, and if anything changes, you know where I am.

## 5. Fewer hours

"Can we just do five hours?" "We only need you for the ceremony and portraits." "What if we cut it down?"

Sometimes fewer hours is right and I say so. Sometimes it quietly breaks the photos they told me they wanted. Say which, and let them choose with the facts.

**Email, when fewer hours works**

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> Yes, that works. With the ceremony at [time] and portraits straight after, [X] hours covers everything you told me mattered.
>
> Here's what changes:
> - Coverage from [start] to [finish]
> - [What falls outside, for example "The getting ready and the end of the reception won't be covered"]
> - [New price, once]
>
> Everything else stays as we talked about. I'll send the revised proposal through tonight.
>
> Looking forward to hearing your thoughts.

**Email, when fewer hours breaks what they wanted**

> Hi [Partner 1] and [Partner 2],
>
> I can do [X] hours, and I want to be straight with you about what it changes.
>
> On the call you said [the thing they said mattered, for example "the first dance with your dad is the photo you most want on the wall"]. With [X] hours starting at the ceremony, I'd be packing up at [time], about [gap] before that happens.
>
> So two honest options:
> - [X] hours from the ceremony, and we let the [first dance] go. [Price, once.]
> - [X] hours starting later, from [time], so we keep the [first dance] and let the [getting ready] go instead. Same price.
>
> I'd lean towards the second, because of what you told me. But it's your day and your call, and either way I'll send the revised proposal tonight.
>
> Looking forward to hearing your thoughts.

**DM**

> **Couple:** Could we cut it down to [X] hours?
>
> **Me:** Yep, [X] hours is doable. One thing to check first: you mentioned the [first dance] mattered. [X] hours from the ceremony would have me leaving before it.
>
> **Why:** Yes first, then the one fact they need. Let them choose with it.
>
> **Me:** Would you rather start later and keep it, or start at the ceremony and let it go?

Fewer hours is the one objection where the price genuinely drops, because they are buying less. The scope moves. The price per hour does not.

## The two lines that run under all five

**"I'd rather you only pay for what you need."** Say it and mean it. It is the reason a couple trusts your price.

**"It isn't held until it's booked."** Say it once, calmly, every time. Honesty about the date is not pressure. Pretending the date is safe when it is not is the thing that costs you the booking.
