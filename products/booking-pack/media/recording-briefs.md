## Recording 1. The 15 minute chat (audio)

**Set-up.** Phone call or Google Meet with camera off, audio recorded. Norm has the demo proposal open. The stand-in couple has had it for "two days" and comes with three questions agreed beforehand so the call has shape: one about the second photographer, one about what happens if it rains, one about payment timing. Target 12 to 16 minutes.

**Beats.**

1. Open warm, one line about something from "the call". Ask what stood out when they read it.
2. Let them ask their first question. Answer it in two or three sentences, then ask if that settles it.
3. Second question. Same.
4. Third question (payment). Say the retainer terms plainly, say the figure sits on the page, offer instalments if they would help.
5. Ask the one question that matters: "Is there anything on the page that doesn't feel right for your day?" Sit in the silence.
6. Agree the next step out loud. "So you'll have a read together tonight and I'll check in Thursday." Confirm the date is open but not held.
7. Close warm, under 30 seconds.

**What not to do.** Do not present the page block by block. The couple leads, Norm answers. Do not fill silences. Do not say the price twice.

## Recording 2. The hour consultation (Google Meet, screen shared)

**Set-up.** Google Meet, camera on, screen share of the demo proposal, recorded in Meet. Stand-in couple on camera too if they are willing. Target 45 to 60 minutes. This is the longer walk-through for a couple who wants everything before deciding.

**Beats, following the page.**

1. **Open (3 min).** Thank them for the time, one personal line, ask how planning has gone since the first chat.
2. **The letter (5 min).** Read nothing aloud. Ask whether the recap matches what they remember saying. Fix anything that does not, on the spot, in words.
3. **How I work (7 min).** For each belief, tell one short story from a wedding. Ask if that is how they picture their day.
4. **From booking to gallery (7 min).** Walk the six steps. Pause on the timeline planning and the gallery dates. This is where "when will we see photos" gets answered with a date.
5. **The options (12 min).** Which one was suggested and why. What each inclusion is for, in moments not hours. The price said once, calmly, then move on. If they ask about moving something, move the scope, not the price (Part 4 stance, out loud).
6. **Questions (8 min).** Go through the ones they have, then the two or three on the page most couples ask. Rain, family photos, second photographer.
7. **Next steps (5 min).** Choose, sign, pay. The booked rule. Agree a check-in date. Ask if anything would stop them deciding by then.
8. **Close (3 min).** Warm, specific, short.

**What not to do.** No slides. No price repeated. No "any questions?" as a filler. No promises about delivery speed beyond the dates already on the page.
