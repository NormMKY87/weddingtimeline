# The Consult Call Run Sheet

One page. Print it and keep it beside the screen. Book 30 minutes. Most of my calls run 30 to 45, because the timeline walk-through is where the couple starts to trust me, and I let it breathe.

## Before the call (10 minutes)

Read the enquiry again. Check the date across every calendar you keep. Open your timeline builder and one recent gallery that looks like their day. Write three lines on paper:

1. What seems to matter most to them.
2. What could change my recommendation (separate getting-ready locations, a cultural ceremony, a big bridal party, an early ceremony, a late sunset).
3. The one thing I want them to feel clearer about by the end.

Start the recorder. Tell them it is running and why.

## 1. Opening (2 minutes)

Congratulate them. Ask how long since the proposal and where it happened. Ask how planning is going. Then say what the call is: "I'd love to hear what you have planned. Then I'll build a rough timeline for your day on screen, and we'll see how much coverage it actually needs. I'll send you everything afterwards whether you book me or not." Close the opening with: "Any questions before I start?"

## 2. Discovery (4 minutes)

Listen more than you talk. Your lines are questions, restatements and one short story. Pull from the ten questions. The minimum you need: where each of them is getting ready, ceremony time and place, reception place, bridal party size, traditions or extra events, whether video is in the picture. Then restate: "So what I'm hearing is you want it to feel editorial without the day turning into a photoshoot. Is that about right?" Wait for the yes.

## 3. The wedding day walk-through (10 to 15 minutes)

Share your screen. Type in the date and the ceremony time. Build the day out loud, block by block, working back from sunset. Let the coverage hours fall out of the timeline, never the other way round. Decide the second photographer on distance between locations and on moments that happen at the same time, never on status. Finish with: "How does that look to you both?"

## 4. Recommendation (3 minutes)

"Based on what you've told me, the part I'd protect first is [their priority]. So I'd recommend [coverage], because that gives you [outcome]." Two pillars at most. No inclusion list unless they ask. Show the one gallery. Name the investment the timeline produced and say it is a ceiling: "Everything we tighten from here brings it down, never up."

## 5. Concerns (3 minutes)

"How does that direction feel?" If something is off, acknowledge it, normalise it only if that is true, then ask one question to find the real concern. Never discount before you have diagnosed. The objections section carries the replies.

## 6. Next steps and close (3 minutes)

If they are ready, confirm the direction, state the retainer and what happens after booking (group chat, timeline planning, a pre-wedding call). If they need their partner or more time: "What will you want to think through?" Then book the next touchpoint: "I'll send the proposal and the timeline today. Can I check in on Thursday?" Your last question is always: "Is there anything I haven't answered that would help you decide?"

## After the call (20 minutes, same day)

Send the summary, the timeline and the proposal. Listen back once at 1.5 speed. Score the call with the scorecard. Write one improvement for the next call. Log any price objection in the couple's exact words. Note every question they asked. Those questions are your next posts.
